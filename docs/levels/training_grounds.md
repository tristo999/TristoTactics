## Design pillars (why this isn't a rectangle)

1. **The space is an argument, not an arena.** A real working training yard, grown not
   drawn: the fence bends around a rock outcrop, the ground is worn bare where soldiers
   muster, the targets back onto an earthen berm because arrows need a backstop. Every
   feature exists for a fictional reason AND a tactical one.
2. **Teach on the terrain you'll defend.** Every tutorial lesson happens on a feature
   the raid then weaponizes. The mud you slogged through slows the raiders. The crates
   you took cover behind — the raiders use them too. Nothing is taught abstractly.
3. **Real mechanics, not flavor.** Cover (+2 DEF, `TerrainRegistry` forest), slow
   ground (move-cost 2), exposed ground (−1 DEF road), blockers, chokepoints — all
   already in the engine, none taught anywhere. This map is where they enter the game.
4. **Three lanes, three characters.** The raid isn't "enemies appear" — it's three
   simultaneous problems with different shapes, and the player's ONE controllable unit
   must choose where to matter. That choice is the battle.

                 ↑ from the camp

- **The fence:** three structural gaps in the north wall (the breaches); the south gate
  faces the camp; boulder clusters outside the east fence for terrain flavor. *(v4: the
  earlier east "rock outcrop" cliff strips didn't read and were cut.)*
- **The pit** (center): an **open stone sparring floor with four corner posts** — reads
  as a ring, no internal fence. *(v4: a fenced ring inside a fenced yard was redundant,
  and the fence autotiler can't orient a ring surrounded by interior.)* The follow-up
  lesson happens here, shoulder to shoulder; in the raid it's open ground worth holding
  for the bell, not a walled redoubt — the defensive chokepoints are the storage rows,
  the well, and the berm lane.
- **The range** (west): a long open north–south lane; straw targets against an earthen
  **berm** (impassable). Longest sightlines on the map — the archer's lane.
- **Storage rows** (east): crates (blockers) and tarped stacks (**cover**, +2 DEF) in
  staggered rows with winding gaps. Close-quarters maze — the knife-fight lane.
- **Muster ground** (center-north): wide, open, worn dirt. Fast and exposed — the mass
  lane, where the line gets held by bodies (Borin + recruits).
- **The well + trough** (west-center): mud ring (slow ground) — costs movement to
  cross; teaching tile for move costs, and a speed bump in the W lane's approach.
- **The footwork course** (south): chalk-marked tiles by the gate — the MOVE lesson.
- **The cracked signal-bell** (Row 5 retrieval cue, LOCKED): on a post at the pit's
  north gap — the most contested tile in the raid; defended from outside in Act 1,
  slipped past from inside in Act 2.

## The raid (a real battle, not a wave)

**The breach:** horn → all three north gaps splinter at once (engine `spawn` ×3) —
battle music's first entrance. Vael assigns the squad in fiction (*"Elena — your lane.
Borin, the line. Recruits, the pit!"*) and the AI allies actually take those posts.

**Three lanes, three problems** (≈11 enemies, tuned in playtest):
- **West / the range (3 fast):** long open lane — Elena solves it *if it stays open*;
  if they reach the well's mud they slow, if they pass it they're in your soft side.
- **Center / muster (5 bulk):** the wide push. Borin + recruits hold the open ground —
  it bleeds without help. The straight fight.
- **East / storage (3 skirmishers):** they come *through the cover maze* — they use
  the +2 DEF stacks (AI seeks hostiles through it naturally), ranged allies are weak
  there. Knife-fight ground.

**The player's ONE unit is the question:** which lane gets the hero? The map answer
differs by read — the hero anchoring center frees recruits to plug a flank; the hero
hunting the east maze (cover favors the aggressor there) protects Lyra; the hero on
the west lane is overkill next to Elena (a trap pick that LOOKS safe). The pit is the
fallback if a lane collapses: rail + two gaps turns a rout into a stand.

**Failure honesty:** lose = the hero falls. The yard is big enough that feeding the
hero alone up a lane gets him surrounded — the tutorial's own positioning lessons are
the counter. Forgettable-win discipline still applies: victory is brisk, celebrated,
unmarked (Row 5 plants quietly: the bell, the breaches, "we held the yard").
